# Guitar Practice Plan

## Overview

Three sessions per week, 60–90 minutes each. Each session has a different focus but all
three reinforce the same core skills: fretboard fluency, chord tone awareness, and ear
development. These are the three things currently standing between you and the next
level.

The sessions are designed so that no single session is "the boring one." Each mixes
technical work with musical application.

**Every session starts with 5–10 minutes of fretboard note training.** This is your
single biggest bottleneck, so it gets daily attention. Use randomscalemachine.org to
generate a random list of notes, then find each note on every string as fast as you can.
Goal is under 2 seconds per note on any string. Once that's consistent, evolve the
exercise: instead of "find the Bb on each string," try "find every Bb on the entire neck
as fast as you can" — this builds the full mental map.

---

## Session A — Fretboard & Chord Tones

**Focus:** Knowing where you are on the neck at all times and hearing chord tones, not
just scale shapes.

| Block              | Time     | What to do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------ | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fretboard warmup   | 5–10 min | See above.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Chord tone soloing | 20 min   | Put on a simple backing track (start with a I–IV–V in one key). Solo using ONLY chord tones (root, 3rd, 5th) of each chord as it changes. No scale runs allowed. Force yourself to find the 3rd and 5th of each chord in whatever position you're in. This will sound sparse at first — that's the point. You're training your ear and fingers to lock onto the harmony.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Scale connection   | 15 min   | Pick two adjacent scale positions. Take a simple melody you already know (a vocal line from a band song works well) and play it starting in one position, then play the same melody starting in the next position. Alternatively, improvise a short phrase (3–5 notes) in position 1, then "answer" it with a phrase in position 2 that starts on the last note of your first phrase. Use slides to bridge positions. You're training the transitions, not the patterns — so don't run scales up and down. Your existing scales-in-thirds and two-up-one-down sequences are fine as a brief warm-up here, but the main work is melodic connection between positions. |
| Song work          | 20 min   | Work on 1–2 band songs. For at least one, try to identify the chord tones you're hitting in any melodic/lead parts. Ask yourself: am I landing on the root? The 3rd? Does it sound resolved or tense?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Free improv        | 10 min   | Solo over a backing track. Try to apply at least one thing from today's session — maybe targeting the 3rd of each chord, or connecting two positions with a slide.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

## Session B — Ear Training & Theory Application

**Focus:** Hearing what you already know theoretically. Closing the gap between "I can
calculate it" and "I can hear it."

| Block                     | Time     | What to do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fretboard warmup          | 5–10 min | See overview.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Interval ear training     | 10 min   | Use a free app (Functional Ear Trainer is excellent and free, or use tonedear.com). Focus on intervals first — you want to be able to hear the difference between a major 3rd and a perfect 4th without thinking. Start with just 3–4 intervals and expand as they become reliable.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Chord progression by ear  | 15 min   | Pick a song you know well. Before looking up the chords, try to figure out at least the first 4 chords by ear. Start by finding the key (find the note that sounds like "home"). Then try to hear whether each chord is major or minor. Check yourself against a chord chart after. Even getting 2 out of 4 right is progress.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Roman numeral speed drill | 10 min   | Pick a random key. Set a timer for 5 minutes. Write out (or say aloud) the diatonic chords as fast as you can: I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°. Then do it with 7th chords: Imaj7, ii7, iii7, IVmaj7, V7, vi7, viiø7. The goal is to get this to feel automatic, not calculated. Rotate keys each session.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Diatonic 7th chords       | 15 min   | Start with shell voicings (root, 3rd, 7th — just 3 notes). These are the most practical voicings: easy to grab, they sound clean, and they teach you the two most important color tones of each chord. Focus on two families: shells with the root on the 6th string and shells with the root on the 5th string. Play them ascending through the key (e.g. Cmaj7, Dm7, Em7, Fmaj7, G7, Am7, Bø7). As you play each one, say the chord name and its numeral. Once shell voicings feel solid, expand to full 4-note voicings (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th) — these are modifications of barre chord shapes you already know. Search "guitar shell voicing diagrams" for a quick reference sheet; the shapes are simple enough that one page covers it. |
| Song work                 | 20 min   | Band songs. For at least one song, write out the roman numeral analysis before you play it. Note any chords that don't fit the key — these are worth understanding (borrowed chords, secondary dominants, etc. — don't worry about naming them yet, just notice them).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Free improv               | 10 min   | Put on a backing track in a key you're less comfortable with (not G or C). Noodle, explore, make mistakes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

## Session C — Musicality & Repertoire

**Focus:** Making it sound like music. Phrasing, dynamics, rhythm, and applying
everything in a playing context.

| Block                       | Time     | What to do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fretboard warmup            | 5–10 min | See overview.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Lick/phrase stealing        | 15 min   | Learn one short lick or phrase (4–8 notes) from a guitarist you like — Knopfler, Mayer, or whoever you're into that week. Learn it by ear if you can, tab if you must. Then: play it in a different position on the neck. Play it over a different chord. Change 1–2 notes to make it your own. This is how vocabulary gets built.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Rhythm fundamentals         | 8 min    | Metronome work on your 16th note grid. Start by strumming all 16th notes on a muted chord (chuka-chuka-chuka-chuka), accenting only beat 1. When that's tight, move the accent to the "and" of beat 2, then the "e" of beat 3, etc. This trains your internal subdivision and directly improves your muting and funk rhythm. For a great walkthrough of this approach, see Andrew Clarke's video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zTdnTJBaI8o&t=156s                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Rhythm application          | 7 min    | Take one song (band repertoire or something you're learning) and play ONLY the rhythm part. Focus on precision: are your mutes clean? Are you locking with the kick drum? If a song has a syncopated part, slow it down to 60% and nail the placement before speeding up.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Triad & chord embellishment | 15 min   | Take a chord progression (from a band song or one you make up). Play it with basic barre/open chords first. Then embellish using these two core techniques: (1) Sus embellishments — on any major chord, add and release a sus4 (pinky one fret above the 3rd) or sus2 (lift the 3rd). These are classic Stones/Hendrix moves and work on almost any major shape. (2) Triad walks — use your diatonic triads to walk between chords. Going from C to Am? Play the C triad shape, slide up to Dm, to Em, land on Am. You've just turned a static chord change into a melodic walk. Start with these two techniques as your vocabulary, then experiment from there. |
| Full song play-through      | 15 min   | Play 1–2 band songs start to finish at full speed with a backing track. No stopping to fix mistakes — play through like it's a gig. This builds performance stamina and teaches you to recover from errors.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Free improv / songwriting   | 15 min   | Either solo over a backing track or try to write a short progression with a melody. Record it on your phone even if it's rough — you'll want to listen back later.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

## Progression Guide

**How to know when to advance:**

- **Fretboard notes:** When you can name any note on any string in under 2 seconds
  consistently, shift this warmup to chord tone location (e.g., "find all the 3rds of a
  Dm chord across the neck"). This is the natural next step and feeds directly into your
  chord tone soloing.
- **Chord tone soloing:** When root-3rd-5th targeting feels natural, add the 7th. When
  that's comfortable, start mixing in passing tones from the scale between chord tones.
- **Ear training intervals:** When you can reliably identify all intervals within an
  octave, shift to chord quality recognition (major, minor, dominant 7, major 7).
- **Scale positions:** When you can connect all 5 positions fluidly, stop practicing
  them as isolated boxes entirely. Instead, practice improvising while deliberately
  moving through positions mid-phrase.
- **Roman numeral speed:** When you can rattle off diatonic chords in any key in under
  30 seconds, start applying this in real time — analyze chord progressions on the fly
  while listening to songs.
- **Diatonic 7th chords:** When the shapes and arpeggios are solid, start using them in
  your chord embellishment block and in your soloing (arpeggiate the 7th chord over each
  change).

**Things to add later (not yet, but within 3–6 months):**

- Modes: Once your major scale positions and chord tone awareness are solid, modes will
  click much faster. You'll understand Dorian not as "a new scale to memorize" but as
  "I'm playing over a ii chord and emphasizing its chord tones."
- CAGED system integration: Connects chord shapes to scale positions and arpeggios
  across the neck. Powerful once your fretboard knowledge is solid.
- Recording yourself: Start recording your improv sessions (even just phone voice
  memos). Listen back the next day. You'll hear things you don't notice while playing.

## General Notes

- **Metronome:** Keep using it for technical blocks (fretboard, scales, arpeggios). For
  improv and song blocks, a drum loop or backing track is better — it's more musical and
  teaches you to lock in with a groove rather than a click.
- **Key rotation:** Don't always practice in G. Rotate through keys each week.
  Prioritize keys your band songs are in, but also spend time in keys that feel
  unfamiliar (Eb, Ab, Bb, etc.).
- **The "monotone solo" fix:** This is primarily a chord tone awareness problem, not a
  scale knowledge problem. When you solo by running up and down a scale, everything
  sounds the same because you're not responding to the harmony changing underneath you.
  The chord tone soloing exercise in Session A is the single most impactful thing you
  can do about this. It will feel limiting at first — embrace that constraint.
- **The maj7 slip:** You mixed up the major 7th (B in the key of C) with the 6th (A). A
  quick way to always get it right: the major 7th is one half step below the root. C
  major 7 = B. G major 7 = F#. This becomes important as you work with 7th chords more.
- **ii-V-I:** Now that you know this progression, start listening for it everywhere.
  It's in countless pop and rock songs disguised as things like Dm–G–C. When you hear
  it, you'll start to feel how the ii "sets up" the V which "resolves" to the I. This
  harmonic gravity is what makes chord progressions feel like they're going somewhere.
